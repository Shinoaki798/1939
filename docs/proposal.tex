\section*{Introduction}
A language model's knowledge cutoff is usually described as a wall. I suspect
it is a \textbf{membrane with structure}: some post-cutoff concepts are
unreachable, others are assembled from parts the model already has and are
half predictable. To characterise it I will train a \textbf{decoder-only
Transformer language model} from random initialisation on text ending before the Second
World War --- American and German press, the German in the original, because the
country that started the war kept its own record --- and ask three questions. \textbf{RQ1 (permeability):} which classes of
post-1939 concepts can a pre-1939 model reach? \textbf{RQ2 (perspective):}
what does reading the German press change --- measured against an otherwise
identical twin trained on English only? \textbf{RQ3 (foresight):} how
accurately does a model that has read everything up to June 1939 predict what
happened next --- the war, a third Roosevelt term, nuclear weapons --- against
what informed people believed at the time? Deep learning is the
instrument: these quantities exist only in a trained model's next-token
distribution. Training ends \textbf{30 June 1939}; July--August 1939 is an
\textbf{embargo}, excluded from training and scoring.

\section*{Background \& Related Work}
Language models degrade on text written after their training window
\citep{lazaridou2021}; temporal conditioning improves future-fact calibration
\citep{dhingra2022}; Time Machine GPT \citep{drinkall2024} trains
point-in-time models. Recent from-scratch
models with 19th- and 20th-century cutoffs, including 1939
\citep{timecapsule2025,ranke2025,talkie2026}, aim to \emph{produce} such
models; this project measures the boundary. SemEval-2020
Task~1 \citep{schlechtweg2020}, HistBERT \citep{qiu2022} and 19th-century
models \citep{hosseini2021} measure how \emph{existing} words drift with masked
encoders; RQ1 asks which \emph{not-yet-existing} concepts a fixed earlier
vocabulary can reach, which needs a causal model with a hard boundary.
Gallup's 1939 polls \citep{gallup1939} give RQ3 a hindsight-free human
reference.

\section*{Data Processing}
\textbf{Sources.} All text is pre-cutoff and in the original language. \emph{English:} American Stories \citep{dell2023},
article-level text from Chronicling America, is the backbone and sole source
of scored sets; training-only additions: Chronicling America batch OCR for the
1930s \citep{chronam2026}, the Congressional
Record \citep{gentzkow2018}, the Federal Register \citep{fedreg} and case law
\citep{cap2024} ($\le$10\%), and public-domain books
\citep{locpdbooks} ($\le$12\%). \emph{German:} newspapers from Europeana
\citep{europeana2015} and the Deutsches Zeitungsportal \citep{ddb2024}, deduplicated by title and date, plus the NSDAP daily
\emph{V\"olkischer Beobachter} (1925, 1930) \citep{vb1925};
German is $\le$25\% of seen tokens per period. \emph{Science} ($\le$10\%, no recency weighting):
JSTOR Early Journal Content \citep{jstorejc}, Internet Archive scans of \emph{Annalen der Physik},
\emph{Scientific American}, \emph{Nature} and the 1911 Britannica \citep{iascience}, and the JFM
mathematics reviews \citep{jfm}.
\textbf{Recency weighting}, as the model must live in 1939: 1934--39 is
seen twice, the rest of the 1930s once; the 1920s fill the remainder ($\le$35\%); pre-1920 $\le$8\%;
within a period, weight $\propto\exp(-(1939-\text{year})/5)$.
\textbf{Splits} by publication date, never shuffled: a fixed 2\% of every year
1900--1955, per language, is held out; \textsc{Train} $=$
the rest $\le$1939-06-30; \textsc{Val} $=$ a disjoint 2\% $\le$ cutoff;
\textsc{Test-A/B/C} $=$ held-out 1930--38 / 1939-09--45 / 1946--55.
\textbf{Cleaning}: (1)~language filter (stopword vote); (2)~MinHash dedup of reprints (word 5-grams, Jaccard
$\ge$0.8) before splitting; (3)~OCR quality per language: period-lexicon hit
rate $\ge$0.75 and word-like share $\ge$0.70, $\ge$50 tokens; (4)~C1 coinages are
screened and counted; pre-cutoff hits (surnames, OCR noise) are period text and stay.
\textbf{Format:} 48k BPE trained on native pre-cutoff English and
German; 4096-token sequences; all numbers are bits-per-byte. \textbf{RQ1 probe set:} $\sim$200 post-cutoff
terms in four classes (C1 coinage \textit{radar}; C2 compositional
\textit{atomic bomb}; C3 new sense \textit{blitz}; C4 new association
\textit{Hiroshima}), each with a frequency-matched control in $\ge$5 contexts,
frozen in advance. \textbf{RQ3
propositions:} $\ge$120 binary propositions resolved after 1939-09-01, in
three tiers by what was knowable in June 1939 --- \emph{near} (war in Europe
this year), \emph{mid} (a third Roosevelt term, a chain reaction), \emph{far}
(a fission weapon used) --- each with same-topic US (English) and German contexts
from June and late August 1939 (embargo as context only), three wordings each,
English continuations, and the Gallup figure where polled.

\section*{Architecture}
A \textbf{decoder-only Transformer} (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}, bottom), a next-token
predictor written from scratch and trained from random initialisation; decoder-only because every
measurement is the probability of unseen text, which a masked encoder does not give. \textbf{Two runs, one architecture:} the main
model (English+German) and, for RQ2 only, a \textbf{twin} identical in size, tokenizer,
token budget and schedule, its German slice replaced by English of the same periods. \textbf{Size:}
349M parameters on 12B seen tokens, no text seen more than twice; $\approx$3$\times$10$^{19}$
FLOPs per run: $\sim$130\,h on the RTX~5080 or $\sim$22\,h on a rented H100. \textbf{Measurement.}
RQ1: for term class $c$, $\Delta_c=\mathbb{E}_{t\in\mathcal{T}_c}[s(t)]-
\mathbb{E}_{t\in\mathcal{C}_c}[s(t)]$, $s$ the surprisal in fixed contexts,
$\mathcal{C}_c$ matched controls, plus a C4 forced choice (naval station vs.\ attack) that doubles as a
contamination check. RQ3: forced choice between two continuations, the normalised log-likelihood
ratio as probability, Brier per tier, perspective and window against chance,
the $n$-gram floor and Gallup. RQ2: the same batteries on the twin; the paired difference is the
German-press effect. All with bootstrap 95\% CIs. \textbf{Success:} $\Delta_{C2}$
significantly smallest and a step at 1939 (RQ1); mid-tier Brier better than
chance and not worse than Gallup (RQ3); a significant paired difference on
the English batteries --- US-context propositions, $\Delta_c$, per-year bpb (RQ2).

\section*{Baseline Model}
With the same split, tokenizer and budget:
a \textbf{vanilla RNN}, an \textbf{LSTM} and a \textbf{GRU} language model
(2 layers, $\sim$50M parameters, next-token objective), which justify the
Transformer; a \textbf{modified Kneser--Ney 5-gram} (KenLM
defaults) is the non-neural floor. \textbf{Plan} (individual): corpus frozen 20
Oct; main Transformer 22--31 Oct; twin and baselines 1--12 Nov.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
Pre-1939 newspapers carry the racial, ethnic and gender language of their
time; the model reproduces it, documented, not sanitised. German press after
1933 was state-controlled: the ``German perspective'' is what Germans were
permitted to read, not a verdict on a nation. In
collection, OCR noise concentrates in older and Fraktur text. Sources are public domain or openly licensed; the model is
released non-commercially, not for querying the private people the papers name.
